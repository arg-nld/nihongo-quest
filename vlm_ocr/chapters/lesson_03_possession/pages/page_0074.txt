Using this concept in conversation is easy. If someone asks, こre wa dare no desu か
(Whose is this?) as they hold an object in their hand, you can use any of the following
answers:

Examples Answers
1. Watashi no desu.	It's mine.
2. Anata no desu.	It's yours.
3. Tanaか san no desu.	It's Mr. Tanaka's.
4. Kaのjo no desu.	It's hers.
5. おbaあ san no desu.	It's grandmother's.
6. Kaれ no desu.	It's his.
7. おかあ san no desu.	It's mother's.

Showing possession of objects using no
In the prior grammar section, you learned that the particle no is used to change words into
their possessive form. The possessive form can be used by itself or followed by another
word to show ownership. Try thinking of no as the glue the holds nouns together. You add
objects after the possessive form as follows:

Tanaka	's	car	it is
Tanaかsan no ＜ruma desu.	It is Tanaka's car.

Examples
my dog	watashi no iぬ nu
your mother	anata no おかあ san
her cat	kaのjo no neこ
his dog	kaれ no iぬ nu
father's pen	おtoう san no pen

The particle no is not limited to linking only two items. It can be used to further clarify the
object or item being discussed.

Examples
anata no kaのjo no namaえ	your girlfriend's name
おかあ san no neこ no namaえ	mother's cat's name
